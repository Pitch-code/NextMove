NextMove Privacy Policy

Last updated: 3 October 2026 · App: NextMove (com.pitchcode.nextmove) · Developer: Pitch Code

NextMove helps you turn messages and tasks into one clear, safe next step and warns you about likely scam messages. This policy explains what information the app uses, where it goes, and the choices you have. In short: NextMove has no account, no server of its own, and does not upload your messages, reminders or contacts. The only third parties involved are Google services described below (advertising, purchases and, if you use voice, your phone's speech recognition).

1. Information stored only on your phone

WhatWhy
Scam alerts (a short snippet, up to 240 characters, of a message that looked suspicious, the app it came from, and the warning signs found)To show you why a message was flagged and let you review it later in Activity.
Weekly counts of messages checked and flaggedTo show the weekly safety summary.
Reminders you create (name, optional amount, place, order number or notes, date and time)To notify you at the time you choose.
One trusted contact's name and phone number, if you add one (typed by you)To pre-fill an SMS to that person when you tap “Alert”.
Numbers or links you report as scams, and handled itemsTo show “reported” counts and your history on this phone.
Settings (language, theme, text size, trial start date, Premium status)To remember your preferences.

This information is kept in the app's private storage, is excluded from device backups, and is never sent to us. You can clear alerts and history inside the app, remove the trusted contact or reminders at any time, or delete everything by uninstalling NextMove.

2. Notification access (message protection)

If you turn on message protection, Android's Notification access lets NextMove read the text of new notifications from messaging apps (for example SMS, WhatsApp and Gmail). The text is checked on your phone against common scam phrases. Messages that look normal are not stored. If a message looks suspicious, only the short snippet described above is saved locally so you can review it. NextMove cannot read your past inbox, does not request SMS or call-log permissions, and never uploads message content. You can pause scanning in Settings or remove Notification access in Android settings at any time.

3. Microphone and voice

The microphone is used only after you tap “Start speaking” in the voice safety check. Speech is converted to text by the speech-recognition service already on your phone (usually Google), which may process audio online under its own privacy policy. NextMove does not record or store audio; the resulting text stays on the screen and is not uploaded by NextMove.

4. Advertising (free trial)

During the free trial NextMove shows banner ads from Google AdMob. To serve and measure ads, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address, approximate location derived from IP, device information and ad interactions. This is handled by Google under the Google Privacy Policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services. You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone's Settings → Google → Ads (wording varies by phone). Ads are not shown to Premium users.

5. Purchases

Premium is a one-time purchase processed by Google Play. NextMove never sees your card, UPI or bank details; it only receives confirmation from Google Play that Premium was bought, so it can unlock features on your phone.

6. Things NextMove does not do

  • It does not read your contacts, SMS inbox, call log, calendar, files or location.
  • Calendar entries are added only through your own calendar app, which asks you to confirm.
  • Images you pick are not uploaded or analysed in the current version.
  • It does not sell your data or share message content with anyone.
  • Scam checks are automated warnings, not guarantees — always verify through official sources.

7. Children

NextMove is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

8. Security

Data stays in Android's private app storage, which other apps cannot read. Because nothing is sent to our own servers, there is no NextMove server-side copy that could be breached.
